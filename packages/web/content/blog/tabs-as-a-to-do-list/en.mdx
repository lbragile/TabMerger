<QuickAnswer>

Save your “later” tabs into a [TabMerger](/features) group such as “This week”. Right-click a tab to **Add note** (what to do with it), **Remind me…** (when), or **Rename tab** (a title that reads like a task). Remove a tab when it’s done. Tabs that sat too long get a stale dot, and one button in the group’s header clears them. Then close the tabs: the list lives in TabMerger, not in your tab strip.

</QuickAnswer>

<Section id="problem" title="Every open tab is a promise">

An article to read before Thursday. An issue to triage before stand-up. A train timetable to check once the dates are fixed. Each one stays open because closing it feels like forgetting it. A week later there are forty, nobody remembers why half of them are there, and the one that mattered is behind the rest.

A tab can’t tell you why you opened it or when it’s due. A to-do list can.

</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why TabMerger’s to-do tools work the way they do">

- **The note lives on the tab.** A note is attached to the tab itself, so the reason travels with the link, into sessions, backups and exports, and to your other devices with Pro.
- **A title that reads like a task.** **Rename tab** gives a tab your own title, marked “renamed”, and **Reset to original title** undoes it. “Triage new issues” is a to-do; “Issues · lbragile/TabMerger” is a page name.
- **Reminders you can’t miss.** At the time you pick, the browser shows a notification with the tab’s title and your note. It stays on screen until you deal with it, and clicking it opens the tab. Reminders are set again after a browser restart, so a restart doesn’t lose them.
- **Stale means you saved it a while ago.** A tab saved longer ago than your threshold (7, 14, 30 or 60 days; 30 unless you change it) gets a dot in its group’s colour, and the group header shows **Remove N stale tabs**. Old “someday” tabs get cleared in one click.

<Section id="steps" title="Build the list">

1. Make a group named after the time frame, such as “This week”, with **Add Group** in the sidebar.
2. Drag tabs from **Now Open** onto it. The open tab closes and a copy is saved (Undo brings it back).
3. Right-click each tab: **Add note** for what to do, **Rename tab** for a task-like title.
4. Star the window or group you’re working through, so it stays at the top.
5. When a task is done, right-click the tab and choose **Remove tab**.

</Section>

<Section id="remind" title="Set a reminder">

Right-click the tab and choose **Remind me…**. Pick **30 min**, **1 hour**, **3 hours** or **Tomorrow** (24 hours from now), or set a date and time; add a note if you like. A clock on the tab shows a reminder is set; right-click again to **Edit reminder** or **Clear reminder**.

<Section id="benefit" title="What you get">

Your tab strip holds only what you’re doing right now. Everything else has a reason written next to it and, where it matters, a time it will come back to you. Nothing depends on remembering, and nothing stays open just in case.

</Section>

<Section id="habits" title="Good habits">

- **Write the note as an action:** “Summarise for Thursday”, not “interesting”.
- **Remind only what has a deadline.** Everything else waits in the list.
- **Sweep stale tabs weekly.** If it waited 30 days, it’s rarely urgent.
- **One list per horizon:** “Today”, “This week”, “Someday”. Move tabs between them with **Move to group**.
- For source-heavy work, see [organizing tabs for research](/blog/organize-tabs-for-research); for the full day, [a daily tab workflow with TabMerger](/blog/tabmerger-daily-workflow).

</Section>
