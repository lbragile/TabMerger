<QuickAnswer>

Keep your tabs in [TabMerger](/features) groups, not in open windows. **Morning:** restore a saved session, or open just the group you need. **During the day:** file new tabs into groups and find any of them with <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd>. **Evening:** save what’s open into its group and close the windows. Auto-save and Recently closed catch whatever you forget. It takes a few clicks a day, and you start every morning with the tabs you meant to keep.

</QuickAnswer>

<Section id="problem" title="Why do tabs pile up every day?">

Picture a normal Tuesday. You open mail and the calendar, then a pull request, then the three docs pages it needs. A colleague sends a link you want to read later. By lunch there are two windows; by evening, four. You don’t close them, because some of those tabs are still “in progress”, and finding them again would mean digging through History.

So the browser becomes the to-do list. It was never built for that: a tab strip has no names, no sections, and no way to put work away without losing it. Each day starts with yesterday’s leftovers, the browser gets slower, and the one tab you need is a 16-pixel icon somewhere in window three.

</Section>

<Section id="idea" title="How does TabMerger fix it?">

TabMerger separates two things the browser mixes up: what is **open** and what is **kept**.

- **Now Open** is a live view of your open windows and tabs. It is always first in the list and can’t be deleted, so it is always one click away.
- **Groups** are what you keep. Each group holds one or more windows of tabs, with names, notes and colours, and it stays saved after the windows close.
- **Sessions** are named snapshots of all your groups together, for the days you want everything back at once.

Because keeping no longer depends on leaving a tab open, you can close windows freely. That is the whole workflow.

<Section id="morning" title="Morning: start from a saved set">

Two ways to start, depending on the day:

1. **Everything at once.** Open the **Sessions** section in the sidebar and restore your session. TabMerger asks first, because restoring closes the open windows and opens the saved ones in their place, each window as it was saved.
2. **One project only.** Right-click a group in the sidebar and choose **Open all in new window**. Nothing else is touched.

The step-by-step version, with the reasons behind the confirmation, is in [start your workday with saved tabs](/blog/start-your-workday-with-saved-tabs).

</Section>

<Section id="day" title="During the day: file as you go">

- **Put a tab away** by dragging it from Now Open onto a group in the sidebar: it is saved there and the open tab closes (Undo brings it back). Or use a keyboard shortcut ([the full list](/blog/tab-keyboard-shortcuts)).
- **Let rules file tabs for you.** URL rules copy matching new tabs into a group automatically; see [auto-sort tabs with URL rules](/blog/auto-sort-tabs-with-url-rules).
- **Find anything** with <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> on a Mac): open, saved and recently closed tabs in one list. Every filter is in [search all your tabs with TabMerger](/blog/search-all-your-tabs-with-tabmerger).
- **Leave yourself a note** on a tab, or a reminder to come back to it; [organizing tabs for research](/blog/organize-tabs-for-research) shows both.
- **Clear out copies** with **Deduplicate tabs**; more in [closing duplicate tabs](/blog/close-duplicate-tabs).

</Section>

<Section id="evening" title="Evening: save, then close">

1. Open the group you worked in, then its **⋯** menu. **Merge with current** adds your open windows to it; **Replace with current** swaps the saved windows for the open ones.
2. Close the windows. The tabs are in the group, where tomorrow’s morning step will find them.
3. Happy with how all your groups look? **Save current session** (the bookmark button next to **Sessions**) to snapshot them under a name such as “Monday plan”.

The habit that makes it work: **save the group, close the windows.** The fewer windows you carry into tomorrow, the faster the browser and the shorter the hunt.

</Section>

<Section id="net" title="The safety net: for the days you forget">

Nobody saves every evening. Two features cover the gap:

- **Auto-save open tabs** (Settings → Data) saves your open tabs as a new group on a schedule, or when your open tabs pass a number you choose.
- **Recently closed** keeps the tabs and windows you close, by day, and <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> with `closed:` searches them.

How both work, and why they behave as they do, is in [never lose a tab again](/blog/never-lose-a-tab-again). For a browser crash, see [getting your tabs back after a crash](/blog/restore-tabs-after-crash).

</Section>
