<QuickAnswer>

Give each research project its own [TabMerger](/features) group, and add a **note** to every source you’ll use, saying why it matters. Set a **reminder** on the ones you need to revisit, save a **session** to pause the whole project and bring it back in one click, and **export** the group to a file when you write up your references.

</QuickAnswer>

<InstallCta>Turn forty source tabs into an annotated, searchable library. Free for Chrome, Edge and Firefox.</InstallCta>

<Section id="project" title="How do I organise a research project’s tabs?">

1. Click **Add Group** and name it after the project, say “Thesis sources”.
2. Open the group’s **⋯** menu and choose **Merge with current**: every tab you have open is saved into it.
3. Close the windows when you’re done for the day. The group keeps everything, and <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> finds any source by title or address later.

Keep one window per strand (papers, data, background) inside the group, and give each a name. Half the battle with research tabs is deciding what’s worth keeping; our [tabs vs bookmarks guide](/blog/tabs-vs-bookmarks) helps you decide.

</Section>

<Section id="notes" title="How do I annotate sources and come back to them?">

Right-click any saved tab:

- **Add note**: why this source matters. A note icon marks tabs that have one.
- **Remind me…**: 30 minutes, 1 hour, 3 hours, tomorrow, or a date and time you pick, with an optional note. TabMerger shows a browser notification when it’s due.
- **Rename tab**: replace a cryptic page title (“[1706.03762]”) with something you’ll search for.

<Section id="sessions" title="How do I pause a whole project and come back later?">

**Save current session** (in the sidebar’s Sessions section) takes a named snapshot of all your saved groups; **Restore session** brings them back together. Use it at milestones, like “Before the lit review”, so you can return to that exact set later. Free keeps 3 sessions in the browser; [Pro](/pricing) keeps unlimited sessions and syncs them between your devices, end-to-end encrypted. More on sessions in [what a session manager does](/blog/browser-session-manager).

</Section>

<Section id="export" title="How do I export my sources when I write up?">

Open **Settings → Data** and press **Export JSON**. The file lists every group, window and tab with its title and address, ready to turn into a reference list with a short script or a text editor. Details and a field-by-field example are in [how to export your open tabs](/blog/export-open-tabs).
