<QuickAnswer>

All three browsers now have **tab groups, tab search, vertical tabs and split view**. Chrome leads on saving and syncing groups, Edge puts idle tabs to sleep by default, and Firefox is the only one that closes duplicate tabs on its own. None of them searches your saved and closed tabs, auto-saves your windows or works across browsers, and that’s the layer [TabMerger](/features) adds, identically, to Chrome, Edge and Firefox.

<Section id="turn-on" title="Which built-in settings are worth turning on?">

<BrowserPanels section="turn-on">
<BrowserPanel browser="chrome">

1. **Memory Saver**: Settings → Performance (`chrome://settings/performance`). It’s off in a fresh profile.
2. **Vertical tabs**, if you keep many open: right-click the tab strip and choose **Show Tabs Vertically**, or Settings → Appearance → Tab position.
3. **Tab Search**: <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>A</Kbd>, or type `@tabs` in the address bar.

</BrowserPanel>
<BrowserPanel browser="edge">

1. **Sleeping tabs** are on already: adjust the delay under Settings → System and performance → Performance.
2. Edge 158 also has **vertical tabs** and **Search tabs** (both in its interface text); Microsoft’s shortcut list gives Search tabs no key.

<AnnotatedFigure src="/blog/manage-too-many-tabs/edge-sleeping-tabs.webp" alt="Edge Settings, Performance, with Automatically put tabs to sleep on and set to 15 minutes" width={1600} height={855} boxes={[{ id: 'sleeping-tabs' }]} source="edge://settings · Edge 158.0.4370.33">Edge’s sleeping tabs, on by default: inactive tabs sleep after 15 minutes.</AnnotatedFigure>

</BrowserPanel>
<BrowserPanel browser="firefox">

1. **Vertical tabs**: right-click the sidebar and choose **Turn on Vertical Tabs**.
2. **Close Duplicate Tabs** and **Unload Tab** are on every tab’s right-click menu.
3. **List all tabs**: <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>A</Kbd>.
