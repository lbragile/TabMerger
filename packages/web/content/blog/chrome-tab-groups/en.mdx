<Section id="basics" title="How do Chrome tab groups work?">

A tab group is a labelled, coloured run of tabs in one window.

1. Right-click a tab and choose **Add tab to group** → **New group** (or an existing one).
2. Name the group and pick a colour. Right-click the name later to rename, recolour or add a new tab to it.
3. Drag a tab into the group to add it; right-click a tab → **Remove from group** to take it out.
4. Click the group’s name to collapse it to a single label, and again to expand it.

From the keyboard: press <Kbd>F6</Kbd> until the tabs are focused, <Kbd>Tab</Kbd> to the group, then the Menu key opens the group menu. <Kbd>Space</Kbd> or <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> collapses it, and <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>←</Kbd>/<Kbd>→</Kbd> moves it.

<Section id="save" title="How do I save a tab group and get it back?">

**Close group** is Chrome’s save button. The tabs close, the group doesn’t: Chrome keeps it and lists it in two places.

- **Bookmarks bar:** a grid icon with the group’s name. Click it to reopen.
- **⋮ menu → Tab groups:** your saved groups.

Two settings under **Settings → Appearance** control the bookmarks-bar part. Both were on in a fresh Chrome 157 profile.

<Section id="sync" title="Do tab groups sync between devices?">

Yes, through your Google Account. Chrome’s help is explicit about the side effect: when you **delete** a group, or **ungroup** its tabs, the group is removed on your device *and* on every other device signed in with the same account. The **Automatically pin new tab groups created on any device** setting also pins groups you made elsewhere.

What that means in practice:

- Groups you make on one computer can turn up on another signed in to the same account; that’s what the pin setting refers to.
- Cleaning up on a laptop deletes the same groups on your desktop.

Firefox 159 has tab groups too, with **Save and close group** and a **Closed Groups** list; this guide’s steps and screenshots are Chrome’s. Firefox users: see [Firefox tab groups](/blog/firefox-tab-groups).

</Section>

<Section id="limits" title="Where do tab groups fall short?">

- **One window each.** A group lives in one window. Saving “everything I have open” across three windows means three or more groups.
- **Addresses only.** Like bookmarks, a saved group reopens the page fresh: scroll position and form input are gone.
- **Hidden from extensions while closed.** The `chrome.tabGroups` API describes groups that are in an open window: every group it returns has a `windowId`, closing a group fires `onRemoved`, and there’s no call that lists saved, closed groups. A tab manager can’t back up a group you closed until you reopen it.
- **Deletes travel.** The sync that makes groups handy also spreads a mistake to every device.

Our guide to [saving all open tabs](/blog/save-all-open-tabs) compares groups with bookmarks, startup restore and session managers, and the [too-many-tabs routine](/blog/manage-too-many-tabs) shows where groups fit day to day.

</Section>
