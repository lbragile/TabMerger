<Section id="tabs" title="When should a tab stay open?">

Open tabs are your desk: what you’re working on right now. They’re instant to reach and they keep their place, scroll position and anything you typed. That’s also their cost: every open page uses memory, and past a couple of dozen the titles shrink to icons and the tab you want gets lost.

Keep a tab open if you’ll come back to it before the end of the day. Anything else is clutter; our [too-many-tabs routine](/blog/manage-too-many-tabs) shows how to clear it in minutes.

</Section>

<Section id="bookmarks" title="When is a bookmark the right call?">

Bookmarks are your filing cabinet: one page, kept for as long as you like, in folders, and synced by your browser when you’re signed in.

- <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>D</Kbd> (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd>+<Kbd>D</Kbd> on a Mac) bookmarks the current tab.
- <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>D</Kbd> bookmarks every tab in the window into a new folder, in Chrome, Edge and Firefox.

<Catch>bookmarks save one window at a time, keep only the address, and don’t tell you which pages belonged together. They’re great for a reference library, clumsy for work in progress.</Catch>

</Section>

<Section id="middle" title="What about everything in between?">

Most tabs aren’t “today” or “forever”. They’re next week’s project, the articles you mean to read, the comparison you’re halfway through. That middle ground is exactly what a tab manager is for, and why [saving all your open tabs](/blog/save-all-open-tabs) as a group beats both leaving them open and bookmarking them one folder at a time:

- **Whole windows, not single pages.** The set stays together, in the windows you had.
- **Named, coloured and annotated,** so “that research from Tuesday” is easy to spot.
- **Searchable** alongside your open and recently closed tabs.
- **One click to reopen** everything, then close it again when you’re done.

Researching? [Organising tabs for research](/blog/organize-tabs-for-research) adds notes and reminders to this.

</Section>
