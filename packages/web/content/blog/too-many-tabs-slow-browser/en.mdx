<QuickAnswer>

Yes: every open tab uses memory, and enough of them slow the whole computer down. The biggest win is to **close the tabs you aren’t using, without losing them**. In [TabMerger](/features), choose **Merge with current** to save every open window into a group, then **Close all windows**: the memory is freed and every tab is one click away. For the tabs you keep open, turn on **Memory Saver** (Chrome) or **sleeping tabs** (Edge).

<Section id="why" title="Why do open tabs slow your computer down?">

Each open tab is a live web page: its text, images and scripts stay in memory, and busy pages (video, live feeds, web apps) keep using the processor too. When the browser and your other apps need more memory than the computer has, everything gets slower. Chrome’s own crash help lists running out of memory as a cause of crashes, and its first fix is closing tabs.

How much a tab costs depends entirely on the site, so there’s no safe number. The practical rule: if you haven’t looked at it today, it shouldn’t be open.

</Section>

<Section id="tabmerger" title="How do I close tabs without losing them?">

This is where [TabMerger](/features) shines. Two steps, every window at once:

1. Click **Add Group**, then choose **Merge with current** in its **⋯** menu: every open window and tab is saved into the group.
2. Right-click **Now Open** in the sidebar and choose **Close all windows**. The browser windows close; the group keeps every tab.

<Section id="browser" title="How do I make the browser sleep idle tabs?">

For tabs you keep open, the browser can free their memory while they sit unused. They reload when you click them.

<BrowserPanels section="browser">
<BrowserPanel browser="chrome">

Turn on **Memory Saver**: **Settings → Performance**, or paste `chrome://settings/performance` into the address bar. Pick how soon tabs go inactive: Moderate, Balanced or Maximum.

</BrowserPanel>

Check **Automatically put tabs to sleep** under **Settings → System and performance → Performance**, and pick how long a tab waits before it sleeps.

<Section id="which" title="How do I find the tab that’s slowing things down?">

In Chrome, press <Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>Esc</Kbd> to open the **Chrome Task Manager** and look for the tabs using the most memory. Save the heavy ones to a TabMerger group and close them.
