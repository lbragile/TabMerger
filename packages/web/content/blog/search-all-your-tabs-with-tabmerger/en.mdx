<QuickAnswer>

Open [TabMerger](/features) and press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> on a Mac). Type a few letters of a title or address: results come from your open tabs, every saved group and, below them, tabs you closed recently, each tagged with where it lives. Narrow it with `group:`, `window:`, `tag:` or `closed:`, then press <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> to jump straight to the tab.

</QuickAnswer>

<Section id="problem" title="“Where was that tab?”">

You know the page exists. You had it open on Tuesday, next to the pull request. Was it in the work window or the reading one? Did you save it into a group, or close it? The browser’s own tab search only sees what is open in that browser right now; History sees everything you ever visited, with no idea what you kept on purpose.

What you want is one box that knows all of it: what’s open, what you saved, and what you closed this week. That box is <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> in TabMerger. (For the browsers’ built-in tab search, see [how to find one tab among hundreds](/blog/find-a-tab-among-hundreds).)

</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why TabMerger search works the way it does">

- **Everything in one list.** Open tabs, saved groups and recently closed tabs answer the same question (“where is it?”), so they come back together. Each result carries a badge with its group, and its Chrome tab group label when it has one.
- **Exact, not fuzzy.** A result is a tab whose title or address contains your letters as one run, ignoring case. Letters scattered across a title never count. That is deliberate: every result has a highlight, and every highlight is the real match, so you can trust what you see.
- **Best match first.** An exact match ranks above a title that starts with your text, then a word that starts with it, then a match anywhere. The list shows the top 12, so the right tab is near the top.
- **Pick, don’t spell.** Typing `group:`, `window:` or `tag:` opens a list of your groups, windows or labels to pick from. TabMerger fills in the quoted name, so long names with spaces just work.
- **Closed tabs stay out of the way.** Up to 3 recently closed tabs appear under the saved results; start with `closed:` to see only closed tabs.
- **The keyboard is enough.** <Kbd>↑</Kbd> <Kbd>↓</Kbd> to move, <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> to open, <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> or <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> to close. Closing leaves what you typed alone, so you can come back to it.

</Section>

<Section id="steps" title="Search step by step">

1. Open TabMerger and press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> on a Mac), or click the search box at the top.
2. With nothing typed, the box suggests searches to try. Click one to start from it.
3. Type a few letters of the title or address, such as `docs`. Matches are highlighted.
4. Press <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> on a tab: TabMerger opens its group and puts the focus on that tab. On a closed tab, <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> reopens it.

Filters combine with words and with each other. Use quotes when you add words after a filter: without them, the filter takes the rest of the line, so `group:Work pull` looks for a group called “Work pull”. Picking from the list adds the quotes for you.

</Section>

<Section id="panel" title="After the search: hide or dim the rest">

Close the search with <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> and it keeps working in the main view. The sidebar shows how many matches each group has (“1 found”), and inside a group the tabs that don’t match are **hidden** by default. Prefer to keep your bearings? In **Settings → General**, set **Tabs that do not match a search** to **Dim**: every tab stays in place, faded, with the matches highlighted. **Clear search** ends it.

While tabs are hidden, dragging to reorder is paused (“Clear the search to move items”): with rows out of sight, where a dropped tab should land would be a guess.

<Section id="benefit" title="What it saves you">

Scanning windows of 16-pixel icons and scrolling History becomes a few keystrokes. It works the same however many tabs you keep: a group you saved months ago is as close as the tab you opened a minute ago. That is what makes it safe to close windows: anything you saved is one search away.

</Section>

<Section id="habits" title="Good habits">

- **Search the address, not only the title.** `github.com/your-org` or `localhost:3000` finds pages whose titles all look alike.
- **Name windows you search often.** “Papers”, “PRs”, “Docs”: then `window:` gets you there.
- **Start with `closed:`** when you know you closed it; the list is shorter and only has what you need.
- **Pair search with groups.** The better your groups (see [a daily tab workflow](/blog/tabmerger-daily-workflow)), the more a `group:` filter narrows things down.

</Section>
