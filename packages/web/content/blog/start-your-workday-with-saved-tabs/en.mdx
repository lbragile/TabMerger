<QuickAnswer>

Keep your daily tabs in [TabMerger](/features) groups, then press **Save current session** (the bookmark button next to **Sessions** in the sidebar) and name it “Workday”. Every morning, restore it: TabMerger asks first, closes the open windows and reopens each saved window with its tabs in order. Need only one project? Right-click its group and choose **Open all in new window**.

</QuickAnswer>

<Section id="problem" title="The morning rebuild">

Most mornings start the same way. Mail, the calendar, the team board, the pull request you were reviewing, the doc you were writing. You open them one by one, from bookmarks, from History, from memory. Some days the browser restored yesterday’s windows instead, so you start with forty tabs and close thirty-five of them.

Either way, the first minutes of the day go to rebuilding a workspace you already had. And the browser’s own restore brings back whatever happened to be open, not what you meant to work on.

</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why TabMerger sessions work the way they do">

Each choice behind sessions answers a part of that problem:

- **A session saves your groups, not whatever is open.** Your groups are the tabs you chose to keep, already named and sorted. A session snapshots all of them under one name, so restoring gives you your plan, not yesterday’s clutter. (The live Now Open view and the guided tour’s practice groups are left out on purpose.)
- **Restoring replaces, and asks first.** To start clean, a restore closes the open windows before reopening the saved ones. That is destructive, so it always asks (**Close all windows** or **Cancel**), from the session view and from the sidebar alike.
- **Nothing you close is thrown away.** The windows a restore closes go to **Recently closed** (on by default), so a tab you still needed is a click away.
- **Each window comes back as a window.** Saved windows reopen separately, tabs in their saved order. A window saved from a private window reopens as one when the extension is allowed in private windows; otherwise it opens as a normal window and TabMerger tells you why.
- **Saved in your browser first.** A session is written to your browser before anything else, so saving works offline. On Pro it then uploads, end-to-end encrypted, to reach your other devices.

</Section>

<Section id="steps" title="Set it up: save once, restore every day">

1. Put the day’s tabs into groups. Open a group’s **⋯** menu and choose **Merge with current** to add your open windows to it.
2. In the sidebar, press the bookmark button next to **Sessions** (**Save current session**). Give it a name, an optional description and a colour, then press **Save**.
3. Tomorrow morning, open TabMerger, click the session under **Sessions** and press **Restore session** (or the restore button on its row).
4. Confirm with **Close all windows**. Your saved windows open, one by one, with their tabs in order.

<Section id="one-group" title="Open just one group instead">

Some mornings you only need one project. Right-click its group in the sidebar and choose **Open all in new window**: every web page in the group opens together in one new window, and nothing else is closed. To open a single saved window, use **Open in browser** in that window’s **⋯** menu; to open one tab, click it.

<Section id="benefit" title="What you get back">

The rebuild becomes one click and one confirmation. You start from the tabs you planned to use, in the windows you arranged, instead of from whatever survived yesterday. And because a restore only reopens what you saved, the clutter stays gone.

</Section>

<Section id="habits" title="Good habits">

- **Keep one session per routine:** “Workday”, “Writing”, “Monday planning”. Fewer, clearer sessions beat a long list.
- **Refresh a session when your setup changes.** A session is a snapshot: edits to your groups afterwards don’t change it. Save a new one and delete the old.
- **Close the day with your groups, not your windows.** Add today’s new tabs to their groups (see [a daily tab workflow](/blog/tabmerger-daily-workflow)), then close the windows.
- **Use a session for whole-day switches, a group for one project.** Restoring is for “start my day”; **Open all in new window** is for “open this project next to what I have”.

</Section>
