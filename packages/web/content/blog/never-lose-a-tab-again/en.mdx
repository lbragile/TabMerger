<QuickAnswer>

Turn on **Settings → Data → Auto-save open tabs** in [TabMerger](/features) and pick a schedule, a tab count, or both. From then on, your open tabs are saved as a new group automatically, skipping anything already saved. Closed something by mistake? <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> and `closed:` finds every tab and window you closed recently, and reopens a whole window in one click.

</QuickAnswer>

<Section id="problem" title="How tabs actually get lost">

It rarely happens in one dramatic crash. More often it’s small: you close the wrong window, the one with the thirty tabs from this morning’s research. An update restarts the browser and only one of three windows comes back. You tidy up on Friday and on Monday need the page you closed. By then the tab is somewhere in History, between hundreds of other pages, if you can remember what it was called.

Saving by hand solves it, on the days you remember. Auto-save is for the other days.

For getting tabs back after a crash or forced restart, with each browser’s own tools, see [how to get your tabs back after a crash](/blog/restore-tabs-after-crash). This guide is about making sure you always have a copy.

</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why auto-save works the way it does">

Each rule in TabMerger’s auto-save is there to keep the copy useful, not just present:

- **It saves into a new group**, named after the time (“Auto-saved Nov 21, 8:28 PM”), with whole windows and tab order kept. Nothing you saved before is changed. Each new auto-save gets a different colour from the last one, so they are easy to tell apart.
- **Two triggers, because tabs pile up in two ways.** A **schedule** (every 15 or 30 minutes, every hour, 2 or 4 hours, or daily) covers steady work. A **tab count** (20, 30, 50, 75 or 100 open tabs) covers the research burst that outgrows any schedule.
- **A burst saves once.** The count trigger fires when you go above it, then waits until you are back at or below it, and at least 10 minutes, before it can fire again. Opening ten tabs in a row doesn’t make ten groups.
- **No duplicates.** A tab whose address is already in any of your groups is skipped, and a page open in several tabs is saved once. If everything is already saved, the run saves nothing and tells you so.
- **Private stays private.** Private windows are never saved. TabMerger’s own pages are left out; pinned tabs and browser pages stay in.
- **It doesn’t need you.** Auto-save runs in the extension’s background while the browser is open, and checks whether a save is due when the browser starts. An optional notification tells you after each run; the status line in Settings shows the last result.

<Section id="steps" title="Turn it on in four steps">

1. Open TabMerger, then **Settings** (account menu) → **Data**.
2. Switch on **Auto-save open tabs**. (On Pro it is already on, every hour.)
3. Pick a **Schedule**, a **When open tabs pass** count, or both. Switch on **Show a notification** if you’d like a heads-up after each save.
4. Press **Auto-save now** once to see the result: a new group under **Auto-saved** in the sidebar.

Found an auto-save you want to keep for good? Open it and press **Keep as group**: it moves to your groups and is never removed automatically.

</Section>

<Section id="closed" title="Recently closed: the other half of the net">

Auto-save covers what is open; **Recently closed** covers what you just closed. Every tab and window you close is kept by day, on this device only, and private windows are never recorded.

- Open **Recently closed** in the sidebar to browse by day, or press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>K</Kbd> and start with `closed:` to search them.
- A closed **window** is listed as one entry (“Window · 3 tabs”): pick it and the whole window comes back.
- <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>-click (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click on a Mac) or middle-click a result to reopen it in the background.

<Section id="benefit" title="What it saves you">

The worst case shrinks from “those tabs are gone” to “I lose what I opened since the last save”. With an hourly schedule, a bad restart costs you at most an hour of new tabs; a window you closed yourself is in Recently closed. Getting either back is one search instead of an afternoon in History.

</Section>

<Section id="habits" title="Good habits">

- **Match the schedule to your pace.** Every hour suits most days; every 15 minutes suits heavy research.
- **Add a tab count if you open tabs in bursts.** 50 is a good start.
- **Keep, then clean.** When an auto-save holds something worth keeping, press **Keep as group** and rename it; delete the rest.
- **Still save on purpose.** Auto-save is the net, not the plan: keep named groups for real projects (see [a daily tab workflow](/blog/tabmerger-daily-workflow)).

</Section>
